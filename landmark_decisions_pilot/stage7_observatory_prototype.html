Pilot Observatory Shows 90.6% Amar Extraction but 81.3% Privacy Review Holds

Stage 7 Judicial Transparency Observatory prototype (24 November 2026), computed deterministically from 32 Mahkamah Agung RI decisions (617 pages) in the LANDMARK DECISION corpus. All statistics below are strictly descriptive (n = 32) and make no causal claims.

Observatory headline: Deterministic extraction recovers final dispositions (90.6%), lower-court histories (87.5%), and judicial reasoning (84.4%) with zero schema errors, but 26 of 32 decisions require pre-release redaction under SK KMA 2-144/2022 and UU PDP.

81.3%

Share of pilot decisions (26 of 32) flagged by the Stage 2 privacy gate for street addresses, e-Court emails, mobile numbers, or family-dispute rules.

Civil and Criminal Chambers Dominate the 32-Decision Pilot Sample

The pilot corpus spans five Supreme Court chambers and three proceeding levels: 21 Cassation (Kasasi), 10 Judicial Review (PK), and 1 Legislative Opinion Review (Uji Pendapat 1 P/UP/2026), averaging 19.3 pages per decision (range 6–89 pages).

Structural Extraction Exceeds 84% on Core Sections, Trailing on Statutory Normalization

Under strict zero-imputation rules, deterministic extraction populated an average of 6.5 of 9 schema fields per decision. Statutory citation extraction (65.6%) lags behind disposition and reasoning capture because inline abbreviations vary across chambers.

Observatory Indicators Separate Descriptive Metrics From Causal Claims

Following Workstream C governance, this prototype tracks seven empirical transparency indicators across the pilot corpus and existing IOJO 2024–2025 Kepaniteraan baselines without inferring causal drivers from observational counts.
